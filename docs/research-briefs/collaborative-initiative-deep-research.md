## Assignment

Research how a conversational AI can act as a useful collaborator over repeated
knowledge-work exchanges: introduce relevant new thinking, challenge a proposal
with reasons, deepen an unclear task, and sustain progress without taking control
away from the person or inventing work after completion. Produce the report in
Russian. Study the domain independently of a particular product or plugin.

The practical question is how to choose an intervention from the actual state of
joint work. A fixed persona, mandatory debate or workshop framework is not an
assumed solution. Include ordinary clarification, shared initiative, conversational
repair, constructive disagreement and transfer of responsibility after a stage.
Distinguish an accepted goal from an assistant suggestion and actual permission
from a general preference for autonomy.

### Required questions

1. What observable signals distinguish exploration, evaluation, scope correction,
   execution, a consequential blocked choice and genuine completion?
2. When does a question improve the decision or reveal missing knowledge? When
   does it shift routine work back to the person or interrupt useful progress?
3. How can the assistant contribute an alternative, relevant evidence or reasoned
   disagreement instead of repeating the user's idea? What makes justified
   agreement useful without manufacturing criticism?
4. How should initiative change after the person rejects a suggestion, narrows
   scope or says a stage is complete? Compare preserving the remaining goal,
   proposing a concrete next decision and simply acknowledging the feedback.
5. What should a useful stage handoff contain? Examine responsibility, purpose,
   dependencies, evidence limits and remaining authority. Distinguish explaining
   a future action from actually performing authorized work.
6. Where can initiative become harmful? Cover scope expansion, overconfidence,
   excessive questioning, sycophancy, unwanted persistence and mode substitution
   after tool failure. Identify conditions and counterexamples, not universal bans.
7. Which findings transfer from human collaboration or older mixed-initiative
   systems to current LLM assistants? Which require new validation? Contrast
   brainstorming, document review, research framing and implementation tasks.

### Evidence and stopping boundary

- Prioritize original empirical studies, published methods and primary system
  evaluations. Treat product documentation as evidence of capability, not benefit.
- Separate human–human findings, non-LLM interactive systems and LLM evidence.
  For important studies retain task, participants, intervention, comparator,
  measured outcome and limitations in a linked evidence appendix.
- Include contradictory/null findings and the cost to user attention. Distinguish
  productivity, decision quality, agency, trust and satisfaction when measured.
- Do not infer real user benefit from model role agreement, idea counts or a
  persuasive demonstration. Mark recommendations based on reasoning alone.
- Cover each required question with evidence or an explicit gap. Stop when the
  mechanisms, transfer limits and unresolved questions are clear. Do not fill
  gaps with invented behavioral certainty or expand into every creativity method.
- Date the search. State inaccessible sources and unresolved citation bindings.

### Topic-specific presentation

Start with the actionable orientation and main evidence limitation. Use a compact
table for intervention choices: work signal, useful response, failure condition
and evidence strength. Keep explanations and citations beside the relevant rows
or in short sections rather than long cells. Show a few short contrasted dialogue
examples, clearly labeled as illustrations rather than measured outcomes.

Explain difficult tradeoffs in connected short paragraphs. Put detailed study
records once in a linked appendix. End with testable hypotheses and which claims
need observation with real people, not a prescribed plugin workflow.
