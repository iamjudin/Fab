## Proposed knowledge work

These are proposed independent topic investigations. Project application follows
separately. They have not been commissioned or promoted into runtime knowledge.

### First priority: evaluating collaborative AI agents across an evolving task

The present suite has useful bounded outcomes, but a fresh baseline can pass while
a real conversation still fails. Investigate how to measure task progress,
retention of accepted requirements, useful contribution, recovery after feedback,
and unnecessary demands on the person across multiple turns.

- Compare observable outcomes, transcript review and calibrated human/model
  judgments. Distinguish capability testing from regression protection.
- Cover changing scope, blocked choices, authorized continuation, social endings,
  interruptions and recovery. Simulated users cannot establish actual human value.
- Deliver a compact comparison of evaluation approaches, evidence limits, an
  example scoring rubric and a bounded pilot design. Keep expensive repetitions
  conditional on what decision they resolve.
- Separately apply the findings to FAB-02/FAB-11/FAB-13/FAB-14. Do not infer a
  long-dialogue fix from a passing short fixture.

The current AI-native study names evaluation and intent drift but leaves these
as open questions. A focused addition is more useful than replacing that study.
[Anthropic's agent-evaluation guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
distinguishes outcomes from agent claims and combines evaluation methods. It is
first-party engineering experience, not independent proof of Fab's effectiveness.

### Second priority: shared initiative in human–AI problem solving

Investigate when an AI collaborator should introduce a perspective, challenge a
proposal, ask a question, deepen the chosen direction or continue execution.
Include disagreement, uncertainty, user correction and control over the task.
Compare dialogue strategies by their conditions of use rather than prescribing
a framework for every conversation.

- Deliver a concise strategy comparison, contrasting interaction examples,
  counterexamples and limits of empirical support.
- Explain how to distinguish reasoned critique from contrarian performance and
  useful initiative from scope expansion. Keep accepted decisions distinct from
  suggestions and claims of knowledge.
- Apply separately to existing Brainstorm/Cowork guidance and actual failures.
  The Six Hats study already exists. Repeating it is not the priority.

[Horvitz's mixed-initiative principles](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/principles-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces/)
and [Amershi et al.'s human–AI interaction guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf)
provide a foundation for initiative, ambiguity and recent interaction. Their
transfer to current LLM collaboration remains a separate question to investigate.
