# Collaborative initiative in human–AI knowledge work

Date: 2026-10-14. Method: bounded primary-source web review, not a Deep Research
provider output or systematic literature review. Relevant research was opened
and reviewed selectively. Detailed records and access limits: [source ledger](sources.json).

## Answer and evidence boundary

Useful initiative should be selected by the unresolved work, uncertainty and
cost of intervention. A persistent thinking persona is not sufficient. This is
an author synthesis across older interaction principles and limited LLM studies,
not an experimentally established universal dialogue policy.

The strongest direct warning about timing comes from a small group-facilitation
pilot. The broadest outcome evidence concerns heterogeneous human–AI tasks rather
than long-running one-person project collaboration. No reviewed study establishes
an optimal question frequency or proves that a given assistant reliably recognizes
the end of a stage.

## Shared understanding and questions

Grounding concerns understanding adequate for the current joint purpose, not
repeating every prior statement. Communication effort belongs to both participants.
That is a human–human theoretical account, not proof that an LLM shares beliefs
or that conversational acknowledgment grants action permission.
[Clark and Brennan](https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1990s/Clark,%20H.H.%20_%20Brennan,%20S.E.%20_Grounding%20in%20communication_%201991.pdf).

Mixed-initiative design considers uncertain goals, interruption costs, correction
and direct invocation. A clarifying question can be worthwhile when a mistaken
action costs more than asking. These are principles illustrated through a calendar
assistant, not an empirical cost model for open-ended contemporary LLM work.
[Horvitz](https://erichorvitz.com/chi99horvitz.pdf).

**Derived decision heuristic:** ask when plausible answers lead to meaningfully
different next results. Use a reversible, explicit assumption for routine detail
when compatible with actual permission. State why a consequential answer matters.
There is no measured universal threshold in this review.

| Situation | Useful question or action | Failure condition |
|---|---|---|
| Goal has materially different interpretations | Expose the deciding distinction | Asking for a full briefing already available |
| Adequate input and authorized routine work | Act, with necessary limits | Returning routine implementation decisions to the person |
| Proposed scope or method changes the commission | Explain concrete options before dependent action | Treating enthusiasm for autonomy as unlimited permission |
| Low-stakes uncertainty with a safe reversible option | Make the assumption visible and proceed proportionally | Concealing an assumption that changes accepted meaning |

This table is a design synthesis, not a validated classifier or a mandatory form.
Authoritative tool/action permissions remain a separate constraint.

## Contribution and grounded disagreement

Sycophancy experiments found that user-position cues could distort criticism and
factual answers in the tested assistants. Preference for an agreeable response
does not establish truth. The tested model generations and artificial tasks do
not supply a current incidence estimate for another system.
[Sharma and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13548v2).

Creative help can improve individual outputs while making outputs across people
more alike. A randomized short-story study compared no AI idea access with access
to one or up to five ideas. This is evidence about a particular creative task,
not product-idea generation or extended co-design.
[Doshi and Hauser](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.00506).

**Derived contribution test:** does the turn add a mechanism, relevant distinction,
supported consequence, counterexample or decision-changing question? More options
or stronger disagreement alone need not help. Justified agreement can add value
by identifying why the proposal fits and when that judgment would change.

Two illustrative responses to “add a subscription” show the distinction:

- Weak: endorse it and list subscription tiers immediately.
- Better: explain which recurring value would support recurring payment, identify
  the unresolved use rhythm and compare a one-off alternative if relevant.

These are authored illustrations, not observed experimental outcomes. No universal
requirement to criticize, generate three ideas or use a named framework follows.

## State changes and stage handoffs

The PTFA pilot involved 48 participants in 16 three-person groups, each with two
discussions. It observed useful contributions and poorly timed intervention,
including continued idea prompts during convergence. Ratings were mixed. That
does not establish an overall benefit of six roles or transfer to solo work.
[PTFA](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12499v2).

Interaction guidelines emphasize correction, dismissal and recent context.
Their relevance was evaluated by 49 practitioners against 20 AI-infused products.
This is heuristic validation, not a randomized trial proving improved collaborative
outcomes. [Amershi and colleagues](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf).

The following stage logic is a **testable author proposal**:

| Work signal | Proportional response | What remains protected |
|---|---|---|
| Alternatives requested | Develop different mechanisms | Accepted constraints and evidence limits |
| An option selected | Develop or execute that direction within scope | Selection is not approval of every later action |
| An extension rejected | Remove the rejected extension and reconnect remaining work | Unaffected goal and commitments |
| A stage finished, larger outcome remains | Interpret result and name actual next action/owner | Completed work is not silently repeated |
| A consequential choice blocks progress | Explain the concrete choice and recommendation | No dependent action before the needed answer |
| Agreed small task genuinely complete | End naturally | No fabricated continuation |

A usable handoff should distinguish result, relevant uncertainty, remaining goal,
next action, ownership and authority. The necessary detail depends on the task.
Evidence reviewed here does not isolate which handoff fields improve real outcomes.
That missing causal evidence should remain visible, rather than treating a template
as the answer to every conversational failure.

## Transfer and open evidence

A meta-analysis found improvement over humans alone on average, but weaker average
performance than the better of human-only or AI-only baselines. It aggregated
106 experiments from 74 papers, covering 2020–June 2023. This makes the comparator
important and does not determine how current LLM collaboration performs.
[Vaccaro and colleagues](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1.pdf).

| Work context | Plausible transfer | Evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Brainstorming | Timing, relevant distinctions and diversity of mechanisms | Usefulness to actual creators |
| Document review | Reasoned criticism tied to an artifact | Factual fidelity and author intention |
| Research framing | Clarify evidence purpose and material scope | Coverage and source support |
| Implementation | Preserve accepted behavior and actual permission | Working state and tested result |

Human grounding and old interface principles inform design reasoning. They do not
provide evidence of modern model reliability. A group discussion pilot should not
be generalized to solo work. Synthetic correctness and subjective preference answer
different questions. A pleasant exchange can still leave the person without progress.

Testable hypotheses are state-sensitive intervention, reduced routine questioning,
and a concrete handoff after a meaningful stage. Each needs a comparator and
observation of resulting work, including attention cost and unwanted initiative.
Optimal timing, long-term learning, user agency and value in solo project work
remain gaps. No follow-up investigation was dispatched by this review.
