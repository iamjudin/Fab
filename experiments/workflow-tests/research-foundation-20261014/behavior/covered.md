**Keep both modes, with an optional starter that can be dismissed in one action and a clearly accessible blank canvas.** That preserves the accepted choice and is a reasonable next candidate for this single-session flow.

The [local evaluation](knowledge/sketch-starters.md) matches the audience and task: adults making a quick private sketch after a trip. Among 18 simulated participants, 14 completed with the starter versus 10 with the blank canvas. Eleven preferred a dismissible starter, while three preferred blank entry and rejected a mandatory starter.

Those results are **synthetic evidence**, sufficient to inform a candidate, but they do not demonstrate real-user benefit or a reliable causal effect. Simulated participants have, inconveniently, never returned from an actual trip.

**The next useful step is a small interactive prototype followed by a focused session with real adults.** Keep both modes fixed and investigate the remaining question: does the entry interaction help people begin while preserving their sense of control?

Observe whether participants:

- Understand that the starter is optional.
- Find and dismiss it without help.
- Start and finish a sketch they consider sufficient.
- Feel helped or constrained by the starter.

Use those observations to refine visibility, wording and dismissal behavior. Stop when the session yields actionable interaction findings, and expand testing only if a specific uncertainty remains. The next step is validation of this bounded flow, without reopening the accepted choice or commissioning broad product research.
