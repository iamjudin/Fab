# Attribution and research materials

Fab incorporates and adapts selected methods and helpers from the maintainer's
Wireframer and Fabricator work. Selection and adaptations are recorded in
`docs/architecture/source-provenance.json`; no other plugin is required at runtime.

The bundled method briefs and sectioned research packets are authored research
syntheses, with links to their primary and supporting sources. Preserve those
links, authorship distinctions and validation limits when reusing the material.
They are not official Apple, Google, OpenAI or other vendors' specifications.

MIT covers original Fab code, instructions and authored documentation. It does
not relicense referenced third-party publications, quotations, trademarks or
externally linked assets. Those retain their respective rights. No permission
to redistribute a third-party book or publication is implied by a research link.

Research packets explicitly identify partial citation verification and unresolved
bindings. Package integrity checks preserve their text; they do not fact-check
every claim or establish native-platform compatibility.

The gamification module selectively paraphrases Kevin Werbach and Dan Hunter's
*For the Win: How Game Thinking Can Revolutionize Your Business* (2012), using
the supplied Russian edition (2015, translated by A. Kardash). Its packet
preserves an original attributed synthesis, not the book or private digest.
The book's illustrations and verbatim prose are not included. Referenced theory
and historical cases retain the validation limits stated in the module.
