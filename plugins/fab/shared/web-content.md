# Public website content

Read for a landing or public informational/service-site task. Apply
[shared interface principles](interface-principles.md) to wording and meaning;
this file adds public-page concerns, not a general app-content contract.
It is independent of framework, page count and visual style.

## Make the offer understandable

Explain what is offered, who it fits, what actually happens, what the person
receives, relevant conditions and the next step where those questions matter.
These are questions to resolve from accepted inputs, not a compulsory section
sequence or permission to invent missing facts. Surface consequential gaps while
continuing independent useful work. Do not commission a study for ordinary copy.

Use concrete subjects, actions and the visitor's familiar terms. Headings should
let a visitor recognize the offer and understand each section. An expression like
"Your next chapter" cannot replace an explanation like "Career consultations";
these English examples are not supplied product copy. An accepted brand line may
remain secondary. Do not flatten useful personality into bureaucratic prose.

Tie benefits to the real mechanism and supporting evidence. Do not fill a template
with generic promises, repetitive value claims, invented proof or an empty wall
of testimonials. A section earns its place through additional meaning or function,
not because another landing has one. Concision must preserve needed explanation.

## Make the commitment clear

Action labels identify the next step. Consultation, free resume submission and
purchase are different intentions even if one form handles them. Do not silently
turn interest into consent to a paid service. Make relevant cost, scope and
eligibility understandable before commitment. No particular form or universal
price-block position follows. Preserve accepted project decisions and copy authority.

Before delivering authored copy, check actual headings, support, repetition,
action meaning and material missing conditions. This is ordinary preflight, not
another review document or approval. Layout and implementation are different
outcomes; do not expand a copy task into them. Do not claim understanding or
conversion effects without evidence.

## Basis and boundary

Creator direction and observed iteration motivate this scenario policy.
[GOV.UK clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)
and [user needs](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/meet-user-needs/)
are examples of need-led writing, not commercial conversion proof, a Russian
editorial standard or mandatory government house style. Shared role/grouping
rules stay in the shared principles rather than being copied into each scenario.
