# Realisation and use

Read when technical feasibility, implementation, increment validation, delivery
or learning is the active decision. Use the selected slice and its current UC,
rules, interface candidates and evidence; do not restart discovery by default.

## Technical strategy and early spikes

Choose storage, platform, integration and architectural boundaries for the
slice and creator constraints. Identify which promises depend on unknown
capabilities, cost, privacy or reliability. Use a focused spike for a critical
assumption before fixing the dependent UX; research/spikes may occur earlier.
Strategy is ready for the dependent implementation when critical assumptions
are checked or the promise is explicitly bounded. Unresolved risks may have
a verification plan, but block any guarantee their possible answers can change. A walking skeleton
may provide evidence; it is not a compulsory second product before every plan.
Observed behaviour, environment and transfer limits belong in the evidence.
Do not treat a simulated permission, save or payment as technical proof.

## Implementation

Build vertical increments from entry to observable result, retaining links
from UC/rules to code and checks. Implement the selected scope without silent
additions. Keep cancellation, history and failure policies consistent with the
model. A demo that resets at reload is not persistent storage. Use the actual
chosen environment and dependencies; label gaps rather than asserting readiness.

An increment is ready for its agreed check when it can be opened/run/installed
as needed, implements the slice and passes relevant technical and product
checks. A critical unresolved risk requires its own disposition; it is not
hidden by successful happy-path execution.

## Continuous validation and readiness for use

Choose checks for the question: data integrity, temporal/boundary behaviour,
errors, usability, value or distribution. Include relevant known corner cases,
not every imaginable combination. Inspect real observed results; a passing
schema or test suite does not prove usefulness or rendered behaviour.
Separate creator judgement, technical evidence and user evidence. A finding
may require a local correction or revision of jobs, policy, slice or structure;
propagate it using the workspace impact procedure.

## Delivery and learning

Make the increment available in its real context: personal installation,
client handoff, internal rollout or public distribution. State operational,
privacy, onboarding, support and rollback limits relevant to that context.
A personal tool does not need a public store release to count as delivered.
Readiness means the intended person can obtain it and perform the promised job
under known conditions, with an understood response to failure.

Observe actual use and outcomes, keep evidence apart from expectations, update
decisions and choose the next uncertainty/slice. Do not invent demand, retention
or a learning report before real use. Journals/checkpoints preserve significant
validated or accepted boundaries, not every draft. Existing systems can enter
at this decision when upstream assumptions are explicitly checked.

## Technical dependency route

For promises whose implementation can change UX use [technical dependencies](../../../shared/methods/technical-ux-dependencies.md). An unresolved critical guarantee cannot be silently handed off as feasible. Link evidence, restrictions and recheck conditions; keep implementation choices free when they preserve the contract.
