# Independent research and project application

Read when preparing research assignments, synthesizing results or ingesting
research for project use. A reusable report is independent of a consuming project's
identity, not independent of the conditions that make its findings useful.

## Two content layers

| Layer | Contents |
|---|---|
| Independent research | Topic question, relevant conditions, evidence, mechanisms, comparisons, counterevidence and transfer limits |
| Project application | Consuming project, accepted requirements, selected findings, local assumptions, decisions, calculations and tests |

For new assignments formulate a standalone topic question first. Keep the project
name and advice specifically for it out of the research title and authored research
body. Translate relevant project context into explicit topic conditions: audience,
age, language, region, use situation, intervention and economic unit. Do not remove
conditions just to sound universal. Mark untested design conditions as assumptions,
not typical behavior or established demand.

Project names in exact quotations, source titles or actual studied cases may remain
when they are evidence. Do not anonymize named competitors or sources at the cost
of provenance. This rule concerns the consuming project's identity and decisions.

Prepare the project application separately when project use is requested. Link it
to exact report sections and revision. State which finding is transferred, why its
conditions match, where the match fails, what remains a local hypothesis and what
decision or test follows. A tag records use but does not establish applicability.
For a standalone research request do not manufacture a project application.

Keep topic-level scenarios and formulas in research with explicit assumptions.
Actual project forecasts, accepted product rules and launch choices belong in its
application. If evidence cannot answer a project-specific question generically,
retain that question in the application rather than claiming a universal answer.

## Assignment and storage

Ask the research tool for independent findings. Prepare application afterward from
the report and accepted project context. If a single requested deliverable must
include both, use clearly separated research and application sections and disclose
that it is a combined artifact. Do not mix local advice into general findings.

Reuse the person's existing research library or requested destination. A generic
topic report does not automatically belong in Fab runtime `shared/methods/`.
Package inclusion still requires the selection and applicability checks in
[ingestion](ingestion.md). No automatic new global library or packet schema.

Keep the source original and lossless indexed packet separately from reader views.
Project application may serve as the existing operational brief when it satisfies
that brief's contract. Do not create duplicate obligatory artifacts. The packet
scope describes its existing storage/use boundary, not universal validity. Continue
using supported `project-evidence` or `global-method` scopes as appropriate.

## Usage labels and existing sources

Use optional project tags or the existing usage directory to identify consumers.
Keep a new report's topic-based title and filename independent of consumers.
Source annotation still requires the person's authorization. Agreeing to this
policy does not authorize bulk retagging, renaming or editing existing sources.

Do not rewrite supplied or historical project-named research to fit this rule.
Preserve it and create a clearly attributed independent synthesis only when that
is in scope, with exclusions and transfer limits. Keep previous labels/provenance.
Renaming an existing source requires explicit rename scope and link/collision checks.

## Review

Check whether the research stands on its own without knowing the consuming project.
Can another project determine whether the evidence applies from the preserved
conditions? Check for leaked local decisions, product recommendations or projections.
Then verify the application against the exact source revision and accepted context.
Do not broaden applicability just because a name has disappeared. Freshness and
source limitations still need review at the time of reuse.
